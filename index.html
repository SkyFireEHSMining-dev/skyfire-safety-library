Risk Assessment Matrix

SkyFire FMEA-style matrix for structured risk review.

Visual Risk Matrix

RPN means Risk Priority Number. Formula: RPN = [(Severity2) x Occurrence x Detection] / 100. Severity is weighted more heavily because high-consequence hazards deserve extra attention even when they are less frequent.

Read the visual matrix as a field reference. Severity runs across the top and Occurrence runs down the left side. Detection is the third risk dimension. When Severity and Detection are the same number, the chart cell lines up directly. When they are different, use the calculator below to triangulate the final RPN accurately.

Rating Guide

Use these descriptions to keep ratings consistent from one hazard review to the next.

Severity

Long-term effects scored to the likely possibility.

1: Negligible — No injury, no damage

2: Very Minor — First aid only

3: Minor — Medical, no lost time

4: Moderate — Recordable injury, restricted duty

5: Significant — Lost-time injury

6: Serious — Permanent minor impairment

7: Major — Permanent disability

8: Critical — Life-threatening injury

9: Catastrophic — Fatality possible

10: Extreme — Certain fatality

Occurrence

A score of 1 is reserved for unexpected instances.

1: Rare — Random or unexpected

2: Very Uncommon — Unusual possibility

3: Uncommon — Reasonably possible

4: Infrequent — Wide interval exposure

5: Occasional — Often exposed

6: Recurring — Repeated often exposure

7: Frequent — High exposure time

8: Very Frequent — Extensive exposure time

9: Nearly Constant — Constant exposure

10: Constant — Continuously and unavoidable

Detection

How the hazard would appear to an unsuspecting individual.

1: Obvious — Noticeable even if distracted

2: Obviously Visible — Almost always noticed

3: Clearly Visible — Usually noticed

4: Visible — Often noticed

5: Slightly Unnoticeable — Overlooked

6: Unnoticeable — Missed if unfocused or unfamiliar

7: Difficult to See — Rarely noticed

8: Semi-Hidden — Partly covered or hidden

9: Hidden — Mostly covered or hiding

10: Near Invisible — Completely covered or darkness

How to Use

Use this tool to compare current hazard-related losses against the expected losses after a proposed safety control is implemented.

This is a planning and communication tool only. Results depend on the quality of the assumptions entered.

1. Hazard Profile

Estimate current annual losses connected to the hazard or problem you want to control.

2. Proposed Control Cost

Estimate the full cost to implement the safety control, including materials, labor, training time, communication, downtime, and ongoing maintenance.

3. Expected Post-Control Outcome

Estimate what the hazard profile may look like after the proposed control is in place. The calculator will use these post-control estimates to calculate the reduction percentage.

Note: Post-control outcomes are still estimates. Use recent incident history, near-miss trends, supervisor judgment, vendor information, pilot results, or comparable work-area data when possible. These estimates are meant to be more defensible than guessing a reduction percentage.
